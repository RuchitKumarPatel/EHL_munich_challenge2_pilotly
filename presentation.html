Objective

The trade-off we chose

We minimise est. input-token spend, priced cache-aware, subject to a bound on process friction — because that is the only outcome this export supports: there is no usage field, so tokens are estimated (chars/4), no public price sheet applies to anonymised model ids, so dollars are an assumption, and there are no quality labels, so friction means tool-call exit codes, not answer quality. Logged bill under the assumed default sheet: $412.95 over 1,000 trajectories and 10,845 billed prefixes — but the three sheets we priced spread by 1.78x, so read the ratio, never the dollar.

The signal

What the router looks at

Structure in the traces

The tools block — available before a single token is generated. bash / file_read / file_edit / file_write marks the claude lane (755 runs); apply_patch / shell_command marks the gpt lane (245 runs). Perfect separation, zero exceptions — so a cross-family reroute changes the tool contract, not just the price, and we refuse it. That gate alone puts 41.0% of est. tokens out of reach, leaving 675 routable trajectories.

Honesty

The off-policy estimate — and where it breaks

  • Method — Stratified reweighting, 33 strata over 778 job clusters — bounds, never points. Plain IPS returns 2.9599 for a quantity bounded in [0, 1], so it has no effective sample. We report Manski bounds instead: the friction cost of rerouting 119 trajectories is at most +0.76 pp (0.48 pp identified + 0.28 pp widening for the 0.28% of spend with no support). No LLM judge was used.
  • Cache trap — 92.0% of the re-metered bill is cache reads — inferred from item-level prefix overlap, because the export carries no cached_tokens. A model switch resets that prefix, so every rerouted trajectory is repriced paying full write cost. That is why the token gap (14.79x) and the dollar gap (2.83x) differ.
  • Weakest point — Our tau is too conservative for the frontier test to be informative. Hull -3.83 pp against router +0.76 pp at our spend — but both are one-sided upper bounds, so their difference bounds nothing and we never quote it. The identified delta is a spend-weighted mean over switched runs only, so it cannot exceed the 3.6% of spend we move — no rule at this tau reaches that hull, however well it routes.
Close

We built the gate, then measured that we set it too tight to test. Naming that is the finding.

3.6% of est. spend — all our router moves

Weakness — no point estimate exists and none is coming: the MDE on the best-powered arm pair is 11.4 pp, so every contrast here is an upper bound and no two of them may be subtracted. The comparator at our spend is not one cheap arm either — it is a randomisation between claude-sonnet-5 and claude-opus-5, and the latter prices at $686.55, the top of the cost axis. Tokens are estimated, dollars rest on an assumed price sheet (spread 1.78x), and the outcome is process friction, not answer quality — so the sign of any saving is not established from this export alone. Next step — loosen tau until the moved spend can span the hull, and add a joint job-clustered bootstrap that evaluates both policies inside one replicate, so a gap becomes a quantity we are allowed to name.
